Learning objectives
• Use the basic vocabulary of a statistical study correctly.
• Identify population, individual, character/variable and modalities/values from given data.
• Distinguish qualitative and quantitative information when the source data justify the distinction.
• Organize a statistical distribution and check totals before interpreting it.

Core lesson
A statistical study concerns a population made of individuals. The characteristic observed on each individual is the statistical variable. The observed outcomes are its values or modalities. A distribution organizes these observations so that frequencies and later indicators can be calculated reliably.

Method
1. Identify the population and the statistical unit.
2. Identify the variable being studied.
3. List the observed values/classes exactly from the data.
4. Count effectives/frequencies carefully.
5. Check that the total equals the population size.
6. Interpret only what the verified distribution supports.

=== GOLDEN LESSON — COMPLETE NABIL EXPLANATION ===

1. START WITH A REAL QUESTION
NABIL: Imagine that I ask 10 students: “How many hours did you study yesterday?” Their answers are:
1, 2, 2, 3, 1, 4, 2, 3, 2, 1.
At first this is only a raw list. Statistics helps us turn this list into organized information that we can understand, compare and interpret.

2. THE BASIC VOCABULARY — LEARN IT FROM THE SAME EXAMPLE
Population: the complete group being studied. Here the population is the 10 students.
Individual (statistical unit): one member of the population. Here one student is one individual.
Statistical variable (character): the characteristic observed for every individual. Here it is the number of hours studied yesterday.
Observed value (modality/value): the result obtained for an individual, such as 1 hour, 2 hours, 3 hours or 4 hours.

3. QUALITATIVE OR QUANTITATIVE?
A qualitative variable describes a category rather than a numerical measurement. A quantitative variable has numerical values on which numerical comparisons make sense. Quantitative variables can often be distinguished as discrete or continuous.

4. FROM RAW DATA TO A DISTRIBUTION
Return to our data:
1, 2, 2, 3, 1, 4, 2, 3, 2, 1.
Count how many times each value occurs:
Value x:       1   2   3   4
Frequency n:   3   4   2   1
Rule 1 — TOTAL FREQUENCY CHECK
If N is the population size and n_i are the frequencies, then:
Σ n_i = N.
Here: 3 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 10.

5. RELATIVE FREQUENCY
Absolute frequency tells us how many individuals have a value. Relative frequency tells us what part of the whole population they represent.
f_i = n_i / N.
percentage = (n_i / N) × 100%.
For 2 hours, n = 4 and N = 10.
f = 4/10 = 0.4 = 40%.

6. WHY RELATIVE FREQUENCY MATTERS
When groups have different sizes, compare proportions or percentages, not only raw counts.

7. COMPLETE WORKED EXAMPLE
A teacher asks 12 students how many books they read during a month. The answers are:
0, 1, 2, 1, 3, 2, 1, 0, 2, 4, 1, 2.
Population: the 12 surveyed students.
Individual: one surveyed student.
Variable: number of books read during the month; quantitative discrete.
Observed values: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4.
Frequencies: 0→2, 1→4, 2→4, 3→1, 4→1.
Check: 2+4+4+1+1=12.
Relative frequencies: 16.7%, 33.3%, 33.3%, 8.3%, 8.3% approximately.

8. QUALITATIVE DATA
Suppose 15 students choose a preferred activity: Sports 6, Music 4, Reading 3, Drawing 2. The variable is qualitative. Relative frequencies are 40%, 26.7%, 20%, 13.3% approximately.

9. COMMON MISTAKES
• Confusing population with variable.
• Confusing a value with its frequency.
• Comparing raw counts when group sizes differ.
• Forgetting the total check.
• Treating a numerical label as a quantitative measurement.
• Generalizing beyond the supplied data.

10. INTERACTIVE CHECKPOINTS
A. In a survey of 30 students about their favorite sport, identify population, individual and variable.
B. A value appears 9 times among 36 observations. Relative frequency = 9/36 = 25%.
C. Frequencies 5,8,4,2 total 19, not 20; resolve the discrepancy before continuing.

11. GRADED PRACTICE
Level 1 — Vocabulary: identify population, individual, variable and type.
Level 2 — Organize data: 1,3,2,1,2,2,4,3,1,2. Frequencies 1→3, 2→4, 3→2, 4→1; total 10.
Level 3 — Relative frequency: 18/60 = 30%.
Level 4 — Compare groups: 12/30=40%, 15/50=30%; Group A has the larger proportion.
Level 5 — Error detective: 25%+30%+20%+15%=90%, so the distribution is incomplete as written.

12. NABIL VISUAL SUMMARY CARD
STATISTICAL STUDY

Variable → characteristic observed
Value / modality → observed outcome
Absolute frequency n_i → count
Relative frequency f_i = n_i/N
Checks → Σn_i = N and Σf_i = 1 (≈100%)
