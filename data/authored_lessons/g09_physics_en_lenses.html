1 · Thin lenses · pp. 23–25

A thin lens is a transparent homogeneous medium of small thickness bounded by two faces, at least one spherical; the other may be spherical or plane.

Thin-edge lens

Thicker at the centre than the edges; converging. Parallel rays approach the optical axis after crossing.

Thick-edge lens

Thinner at the centre than the edges; diverging. Parallel rays spread away from the optical axis after crossing.

Activity 1 · Observe the lens shapes

Compare the thickness at the centre and edges of the lenses shown in the textbook's Fig. 1a–c, printed p. 24.

Activities 2–3 · Send parallel rays

Use a narrow parallel beam: a thin-edge lens makes it converge; a thick-edge lens makes it diverge (book Figs. 2–3).

Activity 4 · Reading through lenses

Compare letters through both lens types; a converging lens used as a magnifier enlarges nearby letters, whereas a diverging lens makes them smaller.

2 · Path of luminous rays · pp. 25–28

O is the optical centre; the principal axis passes through O. F′ is the image focus and F the object focus.

Through O

A ray through O crosses undeviated (thin-lens approximation).

Parallel to axis

Converging: exits through F′. Diverging: exits as if from F′ on the incident side.

Through / toward F

Converging: ray through F exits parallel. Diverging: ray directed toward F exits parallel.

Test the undeviated ray through O; locate F′ with an incoming parallel ray; locate F using the ray that exits parallel.

3 · Focal length and vergence · pp. 28–30

The signed focal length is f = OF′. A converging lens has f > 0 and a diverging lens f < 0. Vergence is V = 1/f, with f in metres and V in diopters (δ). Two thin lenses in contact: V = V₁ + V₂ (algebraic sum).

Activities 8–10 · pp. 29–30

Form the sharp image of a distant object on a screen to estimate focal length; measure two lenses in contact; compare converging lenses of focal lengths 20 cm and 10 cm.

Original textbook exercises · pp. 31–33

Selected worked answers below. Diagram-dependent exercises 9–10, 12–13 and 16 must be solved against their original printed figures; their labels are not invented here.

Exercise 1 · Complete

(a) Transparent homogeneous; spherical. (b) Converging; diverging. (c) Optical centre. (d) Image focus F′, converging; image focus F′, diverging (appears to come from it). (e) Focal length.

Exercise 2 · True / false

(a) False: f = OF′, not FF′. (b) True for a thin lens in air with symmetric focal positions. (c) False: a double-convex lens in air is converging. (d) True. (e) False: for converging lenses V = 1/f, so a smaller positive focal length means larger vergence.

Exercises 3–4 · Identify and use

Observe edge/centre thickness or the effect on a parallel beam. A converging lens can magnify nearby small text.

Exercise 5 · Distant object

For an object far compared with f, the image is formed approximately in the focal plane. The lens-to-image distance approximates focal length.

Exercise 6 · Lamp producing a parallel beam

Place the small lamp at the object focus F of the converging lens, at distance f from O.

Exercise 7 · Converging lens f = 3 cm

Mark F and F′ 3 cm on either side of O. For left-to-right light, F is left and F′ right; reverse their object/image roles for right-to-left light.

Exercise 8 · Measuring focal length at night

Use the distant street lamp rather than the nearby indoor lamp. Adjust a screen behind the lens until a sharp image forms; lens-to-screen distance approximately equals f.

Exercise 11 · f = 10 cm, scale 1/2

Draw F and F′ 5 cm from O on the diagram. Rays parallel to the optical axis converge at F′, giving the image of a distant axial point.

Exercise 14 · f₁ = 10 cm, f₂ = 5 cm

V₁ = +10 δ; V₂ = +20 δ; V = +30 δ; equivalent f = 1/30 m ≈ 3.33 cm.

Exercise 15 · f₁ = 10 cm, f₂ = −5 cm

V₁ = +10 δ; V₂ = −20 δ; V = −10 δ; equivalent f = −10 cm (diverging).
