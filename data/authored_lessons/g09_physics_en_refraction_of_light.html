1 · Refraction of light · pp. 11–13

Refraction is the change in direction that generally accompanies the passage of light from one transparent medium into another. The incident angle i₁ and refracted angle i₂ are measured from the normal, not from the surface.

Air → glass

The ray bends toward the normal. For normal incidence, there is no deviation.

Glass → air

The ray bends away from the normal if refraction occurs.

Explanatory model using Snell's law n₁ sin(i₁) = n₂ sin(i₂), to calculate angles for the simulation; the textbook chapter defines n = c/v and illustrates ray paths experimentally.

2 · Index of refraction · p. 14

n = c / v, where c is the speed of light in vacuum and v is its speed in the medium. The index has no unit. Larger n means lower speed of light in that medium.

Water 1.33
Plexiglas 1.51
Crown glass 1.52
Diamond 2.42

Illustrative values from the source table, printed p. 14. The ray simulator uses 1.00 for air and 1.50 for glass as approximate classroom values.

3 · Total internal reflection · pp. 15–17

When light passes from a more refractive to a less refractive medium, there is a critical angle ic. At i = ic, the refracted ray grazes the interface (90° to the normal). For i > ic, total internal reflection occurs.

In the textbook's glass → air example, the critical angle is approximately 42°. A 45° ray in glass is therefore totally reflected.

Applications in the book: totally reflecting prisms (Activity 6, pp. 16–17) and optical fibres (documentation, pp. 21–22).

Exercise 1 · Complete the statements

(a) Incident ray; normal. (b) Reflected ray. (c) Refracted ray; less than or equal to the critical angle; reflection; grazing the interface.

Exercise 2 · True or false

(a) False: a reflecting surface produces reflection. (b) False: total internal reflection is possible. (c) False: it requires passage toward a less refractive medium. (d) True in the textbook's ideal interface model. (e) False: air → glass bends toward the normal.

Exercise 3 · Multiple choice

Index: n = c/v (a). Total reflection: i₁ > ic (c). Speed in glass is smaller than in water (c). Glass → air, refracted angle 60°: incident angle ≈ 38° (c), using approximate n(glass) = 1.5.

Exercise 5 · Two media

Because i₂ > i₁, medium B is less refractive. If the media are air and water, A is water, B is air; light travels slower in water.

Exercise 8 · Critical angle 45°

(a) 45° is the critical angle. (b) At 40° refraction into air occurs; at 60° total internal reflection occurs.

Exercise 9 · Right isosceles prism

Incidence at the hypotenuse is 0°; incidence at each reflecting leg is 45°, above the given 42° critical angle. Two total internal reflections return the emergent ray in the opposite direction to the incident ray (180° deviation).

Exercise 13 · Reversibility

For a refracted ray, reversing the direction of propagation retraces the same optical path between the same two media.
